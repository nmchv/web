# About me

My name is Aleksei Vladimirovich Nemichev. I am a geography teacher, an author of educational projects, and someone who enjoys exploring the world around us, understanding complex systems, and creating my own tools to solve practical problems.

My main interests lie at the intersection of geography, education, technology, and scientific inquiry. I am interested not only in learning about what already exists, but also in finding ways to make familiar processes clearer, more convenient, and more effective.

I see my professional life as a process of continuous development. I do not intend to limit myself forever to one profession or field of work. On the contrary, I want to keep expanding my skills, find connections between different areas of knowledge, and apply what I learn to my own projects.

This website brings together the main areas of my work, its results, and ideas that I am gradually turning into real projects.

## Geography as a way of understanding the world

To me, geography is much more than the study of countries, capitals, rivers, mountains, and coordinates. It is a way of looking at the world as a complex system in which natural processes, society, the economy, history, and human activity constantly interact.

I am interested in how our planet works, why places differ from one another, how natural conditions develop, and how geographic location influences the growth of countries, cities, and regions.

Geographic thinking helps us see connections where at first there seem to be only separate facts. It teaches us to consider phenomena in space, compare places, analyse patterns, and account for the influence of many different factors.

This complexity is what makes geography especially interesting to me. It brings together natural and social perspectives, uses maps and data for analysis, and raises questions that cannot be answered by looking at just one discipline.

I believe that geography education should provide not only knowledge about the world, but also the tools to understand it. It is important not merely to memorise where things are, but to understand why they are there, how they are connected, and what they mean for society and the environment.

## Teaching and education

My main professional activity today is teaching geography at school.

For me, teaching is not only an opportunity to share knowledge, but also to create conditions in which students learn to think independently, ask questions, analyse information, and develop their own understanding of the world around them.

I am interested in making learning more meaningful. I see education as a system in which the sequence of learning activities, feedback, independent work, and an understanding of each student's progress matter as much as explaining material and checking knowledge.

In geography, it is particularly important to combine theory and practice. Maps, diagrams, geographic data, spatial-thinking tasks, and research questions can turn the study of a subject into active intellectual work.

I also believe that digital tools should be used when they genuinely support learning. Technology should not replace educational content or become an end in itself. Its purpose is to extend what teachers and students can do, make information easier to work with, and make some learning processes clearer and more convenient.

I find pedagogy interesting both as a practical activity and as a field for further study. It constantly raises questions that call for analysis: how should learning outcomes be assessed, independent work organised, motivation supported, and data used to understand educational results?

I see my own teaching practice as a source of experience that can be analysed, tested, and steadily improved.

## Digital projects and development

One of the areas I am actively developing is the creation of my own digital tools.

I am interested not only in using ready-made programmes and services, but also in building solutions for specific needs. This helps me better understand how digital products work and what decisions are involved in their design.

For me, a project is a way to combine subject knowledge, practical experience, and new technical skills. Development requires breaking a task down, planning how it should work, choosing suitable tools, and checking whether the result solves the original problem.

I am especially interested in projects with a practical purpose. I want development to be more than an experiment for its own sake: it should be usable in real work, help people, or make a task more accessible.

Many of my current projects relate to education and geography. This is a natural extension of my professional work: I understand the challenges that arise in learning and can look for ways to address them with technology.

At the same time, I do not see programming only as a supplementary skill for a teacher. I want to deepen my development skills, explore new tools, and broaden the range of problems I can solve independently.

### Teacher's diary

One of my projects is a digital diary designed to help teachers organise their work.

The idea is to bring together different parts of everyday teaching in one place: lesson information, grades, attendance, lesson plans, homework, and learning statistics.

A teacher's work is not limited to interacting with students in class. Much time is also spent preparing, keeping records, organising information, and analysing results.

I am interested in how these processes can be organised more consistently and conveniently, so that a digital tool can help maintain a complete picture of teaching and learning.

When developing a tool like this, individual features are not the only thing that matters; the overall logic of the interface matters too. Information should be easy to find, the structure should be clear, and everyday tasks should be as simple and quick as possible.

In the future, I see this project as a foundation for further educational tools that could be useful not only to me, but also to other teachers.

### Geography trainer

Another project is an online trainer for practising geographic names and locations.

Knowing geographic features and where they are located remains an important part of geography education. Yet learning them can be much more engaging than simply memorising lists.

The Geography Trainer is intended to give users a convenient environment for reviewing and checking their knowledge. It lets them focus on particular groups of features, return to material regularly, and use practical exercises to consolidate what they have learned.

I am interested in developing tools that support independent learning. Students should be able to practise, see the results of their work, and understand which topics need more attention.

The Geography Trainer combines my interest in geography with my interest in educational services. It is an example of how knowledge of a subject can become the basis for creating a digital product of one's own.

### Geography notes

Another area of my work is organising learning materials for school geography.

I collect and structure notes on the topics being studied, aiming to present information in a clear sequence that is convenient for review.

Working on notes helps organise knowledge, identify key concepts, connect different topics, and build a coherent picture of the subject.

For me, this project has both practical and intellectual value. Preparing material is not simply a matter of putting information on a page; it means deciding what is essential for understanding a topic, how different facts relate, and in what order to present them.

Over time, this collection could become the basis for a broader learning environment combining theory, practical exercises, and digital learning tools.

## A research-minded approach

I enjoy understanding how different phenomena and systems work. When a subject truly interests me, I prefer not to stop at a superficial introduction.

Research begins with a question. The next steps are to gather information, understand existing explanations, compare facts, identify contradictions, and form a well-supported view of my own.

This approach applies to more than geography. It is useful when studying technology, analysing education, working on digital products, and making sense of social phenomena.

Intellectual independence matters to me, but it should not be confused with believing that I am always right. Any idea may be incomplete, and an initial explanation may be mistaken. I therefore believe that conclusions should be revisited when new data or more convincing arguments emerge.

I do not try to give the impression that I already know the answers to every question that interests me. It is more important to remain able to learn, acknowledge the limits of my knowledge, and steadily broaden my perspective.

That is why I see research not only as a profession in its own right, but also as an approach to understanding the world.

## Technology and artificial intelligence

Modern technology makes it possible to create tools that until recently would have required far more resources and specialised knowledge.

I am interested in putting these opportunities to practical use: developing applications, automating processes, working with data, and experimenting with artificial intelligence.

The intersection of technology and education is particularly compelling. It offers ways to ease routine tasks, organise learning materials, analyse results, and create new ways of engaging with content.

I see technology primarily as a tool. The mere presence of artificial intelligence or a complex software solution does not make a product useful. What matters more is defining the problem, understanding the technology's limitations, and checking whether the chosen approach actually improves the result.

I want to keep expanding my technical skills and learn how the tools I use work. This helps me do more than apply ready-made solutions: it lets me design my own with a clear understanding of what they can and cannot do.

In the future, I would like to develop this area further by exploring programming, automation, and intelligent systems.

## Personal interests and observations

Beyond teaching and development, I am interested in observing how the world around us works, how people interact, and how society, culture, and technology change.

I enjoy exploring questions that do not always have obvious answers. Sometimes they are scientific topics, sometimes aspects of everyday life, and sometimes ideas that arise while reading, travelling, or learning about a new field.

I believe that varied interests help us make unexpected connections between ideas. An idea from one field may suggest a solution in another, and learning about a new subject can change what once seemed obvious.

At the same time, I want not only to accumulate information, but also to think it through, distinguish facts from assumptions, and develop my own position.

## Principles of work

In my work, I try to follow a few principles.

**Practical value.** I want to understand what problem a project solves and whom it can help. Even an interesting idea needs to be tested in practice.

**A systems perspective.** I prefer to consider individual tasks within a broader system. This helps reveal connections and constraints and supports better-informed decisions.

**Independence.** I enjoy investigating questions, learning new tools, and taking my own ideas all the way to a working result.

**Openness to changing my mind.** My conclusions should not become an obstacle to further learning. New information may mean revising an initial position.

**Steady development.** Complex projects rarely arrive fully formed. They usually require successive iterations, testing hypotheses, and continuous improvement.

**Connecting knowledge with practice.** Theory is especially valuable when it helps us understand real processes and create solutions that can be tested.

These principles do not mean that every project develops in a straight line or that every problem is solved on the first attempt. On the contrary, working on my own projects continually shows me how important it is to adjust plans, pay attention to detail, and learn from mistakes.
